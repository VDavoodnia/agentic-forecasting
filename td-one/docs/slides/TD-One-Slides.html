Slide 1

Executive Summary

The opportunity

A lot of mortgages come up for renewal every month, and some of those customers switch to another lender. Today this is mostly predicted with classical models that look at past numbers. They react slowly when conditions change.

Our solution

A system that forecasts, for each customer segment and each coming month, how many renewing customers are likely to switch. It gives a best guess and an honest range. A committee of models makes the forecast, a validation gate blocks decisions the past record cannot support, and a separate layer sizes the range. It writes a short manager report: which segments need attention, and why, with the evidence cited.

Business impact

The method was tested on three kinds of data: a synthetic mortgage book built by the team, real mortgage data from Canada and the United States, and standard public forecasting datasets. On the public data it beat the equal-weight mix and every classical model we tried, on all four datasets. On real Canadian bank runoff the committee alone did not beat a simple average, and in one segment it did harm. The validation gate repaired that harm. On United States mortgage leaving rates, a large language model used with strict gating matched the best models but was not significantly better.

Next steps

The segment forecaster's center is settled: the trailing six-month average is the default, beating the last value on control data and under a rate shock (exp79). The real fitted router was tested and rejected. Finish testing calibrated news signals in the range layer (exp76). Connect the pieces into one monthly manager report. Then test whether an agent reading policy and news can propose at-risk segments a human would miss.

Slide 2

Business Problem and Opportunity

1

Renewal dates are known years ahead. Customer decisions are not. Nobody can say today how many of next June's renewals will leave.

2

Switching depends on many factors. Some sit with the customer: income, family, the rate they locked in years ago. Others sit outside the bank: central bank rates, policy rules, competitor offers.

3

Segments are hard to read month to month. A small segment may have 20 renewals in a month. Its switching rate can swing from 25% to 60% on chance alone.

Forecast before the month arrives

For each segment: the renewals due, the expected switchers, and a range, early enough to act on.

Combine, then measure each piece

No single model reads everything. We test whether a system that combines several models, with an agent in specific roles, does better. Each role is measured on its own.

Drop what does not beat the simple option

What fails the comparison is removed, and the negative result is written down with the same care as the wins.

Proof of concept. The mortgage data is synthetic, built by the team, with known switching drivers planted in it. The method is also tested on public datasets that anyone can download, and on real mortgage data: Canadian bank balances (OSFI M4, 121 months) and United States mortgage leaving rates (Freddie Mac, 63 months). OSFI is the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Canadian banking regulator. Results on all of them are on the results slide.

Design goal: P( A = a  |  N , past A , past N , customer features X , external context H )
What the built system uses today: P( A = a  |  N , segment history )  ·  A = customers who switch · N = renewals due, known exactly

The two formulas differ on purpose. Customer features X are used before forecasting, to draw the segment borders. External context H is used in report text, with dates and citations. A service also turns dated news into monthly calibrated signals. Those signals did not move forecasts on the two real datasets tried (see the negatives slide), and their use inside the range layer is in testing (exp76), not yet part of the built system. H is not an input to the forecast itself. Evidence for that choice is on the approach slide.

Forecast engine

The committee. Classical models are routed by the shape of the data. A large language model joins as one expert only where it has proven to help. A validation gate can veto the mix and use the simple average instead.

Honest ranges

A separate layer sets the range width from past errors, so the range means what it says. A calibrated news signal service exists (7 of 9 checked categories predict realized data). Wiring its signals into this layer is in testing (exp76), not yet shipped. Nothing from news moves the best guess.

Segments and explanations

Segments are defined from customer features, by rules a manager can read and challenge. An agent writes the segment notes. Every fact must cite a computed number or a dated article. A checker rejects uncited text.

Definitions. ETS and ARIMA are classical forecasting methods: ETS follows trend and seasonal patterns, ARIMA follows recent values and recent errors. CQR (conformalized quantile regression) is a statistical method that sizes a range so that reality lands inside it at a target rate. A pretrained model is a forecasting model trained once, elsewhere, on many series, and used here unchanged. Score: CRPS (continuous ranked probability score), an error score for a whole forecast distribution. Lower is better. It rewards a forecast that is close and honest about its width. We also check whether reality lands inside the range about 80% of the time. One rule for every test: a forecast for a date uses only information that existed on that date.

Real mortgage data, and what happened there

DataResult
Canadian bank runoff
OSFI M4, 121 months, 4 mortgage segments		The committee tied the simple average overall (difference -0.2%, not significant) and was significantly worse on uninsured mortgages (+10.7%, p=0.030). The validation gate repaired this: the harm fell to +0.8% (not significant), and at a 3-month horizon the gated system beats the committee by 6.8% (p=0.015).
United States mortgage leaving rates
Freddie Mac, 63 months		Committee plus language model expert ties its own classical version (p=0.69, neutral). This monthly leaving-rate series is the project's honest real-data attrition benchmark (exp65). Pretrained TimesFM was competitive (-12.2% against ETS, p=0.076, not significant).
Honest ranges (CQR)
Food prices, 59 forecasts		The range layer beat the model's own ranges on error (1.2824 vs 1.3035, p=1.7e-04), put reality inside the 80% range 82.4% of the time, and cost 7.8% wider ranges. It is now the default range method.

These are not the same test world as the bars above. The bars are public forecasting competitions. This block is real mortgage data. Three naming notes: OSFI M4 is the Canadian regulator's mortgage balance report; it is unrelated to the M4 forecasting competition. SMM (single monthly mortality) is the standard name for the monthly rate at which mortgage loans leave a portfolio. FRED is the United States Federal Reserve's public data library; CPIUFDSL is its monthly food price index series.

Groups invented by a language modelForecast worse than random groups. The model can write about groups, but it cannot find them.Grouping is for explanation, not forecasting. Deterministic groups with cited notes shipped instead.
Group forecaster ranges (defect found and fixed, exp78)The group forecaster's router never actually ran: a skipped fit step and a swallowed error meant every forecast was the last value repeated. Reality landed inside the range 0% of the time.Fix shipped as the default: ranges rebuilt from the series' own past errors. Coverage 0% to 71% on synthetic control (target 80%), error down 21% (p=4.2e-45). The published grouping cost was also wrong: +0.0018 with the settled center, not +0.016, because the defect had flattered the global baseline. The forecast center was settled in exp79: the trailing six-month average is now the default (control error 0.0899 vs 0.1042 for the last value, p=0.034; under a 4-sigma rate shock, 0.1193 vs 0.1456, p=4.7e-07, range coverage 71% vs 60%). The real fitted router was tested and rejected: on 12 to 29 point group series it has at most 13 past forecasts to learn its regimes from. It stays an option, and its failures raise loudly instead of silently falling back.

All of these were measured under the point-in-time rule: a forecast for a date uses only information that existed on that date. A long negatives table is the price of keeping the positive claims, and their ranges, exact.

Slide 8

Future Work and Opportunities

Segment scout

An agent reads policy and news, and proposes at-risk segments nobody defined in advance. The synthetic data has planted segments, so we can score how often it finds the real ones. This exact role has not been tested yet.

Range layer with news

Calibrated news signals and story features are being wired into the range layer, where narrative information does carry signal (experiment in flight). The experiment that proves it is exp76.

One monthly report

Forecast, segment notes, and cited evidence in a single run. The pieces exist. The wiring is the remaining work. The mock on the previous slide is the specification.

Group forecaster center: settled

exp79 tested three centers head to head. The trailing six-month average won and is now the default: control error 0.0899 vs 0.1042 for the last value (p=0.034), and under a 4-sigma rate shock 0.1193 vs 0.1456 (p=4.7e-07), with range coverage 71% under the shock (a fresh-seed check confirmed the win, p=1.6e-05). The real fitted router lost: fitted per segment, it has at most 13 past forecasts to learn from, too few for its regimes to separate. It remains available as an option, not the default. Grouping cost with the new center: +0.0018 error points against a global forecast.

Real data: loan level

Aggregate Canadian runoff is now the real-data null benchmark every claim must beat. The remaining open question is at loan level: the Freddie Mac loan-level data, which is where the competitor-rate question may still have an answer.

Real data: a pilot

The pipeline consumes segment counts only, no customer level records. A pilot on aggregated renewal data remains the natural next step.

One caveat on the range layer work: an earlier test (exp74) found that story features from news improved range estimates (p=2.0e-11). A later audit found a one-month keying offset in that test. The direction is plausible; the size of the gain is being re-measured in exp76 with the offset fixed, so the deck does not cite it as a result.
