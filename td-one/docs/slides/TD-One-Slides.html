Slide 1

Executive Summary

The opportunity

A lot of mortgages come up for renewal every month, and some of those customers switch to another lender. Today this is mostly predicted with classical models that look at past numbers. They react slowly when conditions change.

Our solution

A system that forecasts, for each customer segment and each coming month, how many renewing customers are likely to switch. It gives a best guess and an honest range. A committee of models makes the forecast, a validation gate blocks decisions the past record cannot support, and a separate layer sizes the range. It writes a short manager report: which segments need attention, and why, with the evidence cited.

Business impact

The method was tested on three kinds of data: a synthetic mortgage book built by the team, real mortgage data from Canada and the United States, and standard public forecasting datasets. On the public data it beat the equal-weight mix and every classical model we tried, on all four datasets. On real Canadian bank runoff the committee alone did not beat a simple average, and in one segment it did harm. The validation gate repaired that harm. On United States mortgage leaving rates, a large language model used with strict gating matched the best models but was not significantly better.

Next steps

The segment forecaster's center is settled: the trailing six-month average is the default, beating the last value on control data and under a rate shock (exp79). The real fitted router was tested and rejected. The range layer tests are finished (exp76): the plain range layer stays the default, and a fusion that reads story features from news is used where those features exist. Connect the pieces into one monthly manager report. Then test whether an agent reading policy and news can propose at-risk segments a human would miss.

Slide 2

Business Problem and Opportunity

1

Renewal dates are known years ahead. Customer decisions are not. Nobody can say today how many of next June's renewals will leave.

2

Switching depends on many factors. Some sit with the customer: income, family, the rate they locked in years ago. Others sit outside the bank: central bank rates, policy rules, competitor offers.

3

Segments are hard to read month to month. A small segment may have 20 renewals in a month. Its switching rate can swing from 25% to 60% on chance alone.

Forecast before the month arrives

For each segment: the renewals due, the expected switchers, and a range, early enough to act on.

Combine, then measure each piece

No single model reads everything. We test whether a system that combines several models, with an agent in specific roles, does better. Each role is measured on its own.

Drop what does not beat the simple option

What fails the comparison is removed, and the negative result is written down with the same care as the wins.

Forecast engine

The committee. Classical models are routed by the shape of the data. A large language model joins as one expert only where it has proven to help. A validation gate can veto the mix and use the simple average instead.

Honest ranges

A separate layer sets the range width from past errors, so the range means what it says. Where news text about a series has been turned into story features, a tested fusion uses them to size the range better (food prices, exp76). Calibrated macro news signals were tested here too and added nothing beyond the story features. Nothing from news moves the best guess.

Segments and explanations

Segments are defined from customer features, by rules a manager can read and challenge. An agent writes the segment notes. Every fact must cite a computed number or a dated article. A checker rejects uncited text.

Definitions. ETS and ARIMA are classical forecasting methods: ETS follows trend and seasonal patterns, ARIMA follows recent values and recent errors. CQR (conformalized quantile regression) is a statistical method that sizes a range so that reality lands inside it at a target rate. A pretrained model is a forecasting model trained once, elsewhere, on many series, and used here unchanged. Score: CRPS (continuous ranked probability score), an error score for a whole forecast distribution. Lower is better. It rewards a forecast that is close and honest about its width. We also check whether reality lands inside the range about 80% of the time. One rule for every test: a forecast for a date uses only information that existed on that date.

Section 1

Architecture (General, Benchmark-Independent)

The figure on Slide 3 is the architecture. This section states the design principles that hold on every benchmark, and the evidence behind each component.

Design principles

1. Point-in-time safety is a gate, not a guideline

No feature, label, or context may use information dated after the forecast origin. Enforced in code (the point-in-time store and its as-of accessors) and audited by a leakage checker. A leak is treated like a failing test, not a follow-up. Source: docs/AGENTS.md, rule 1.

2. The language model never outputs numbers

Language models emit structure only: regime labels, confidences, selections, directions, explanations. Every number in a forecast comes from statistical code. Source: docs/AGENTS.md, research conventions.

3. Gates before complexity

Every added component must first pass a gate that can refuse it: the do-nothing gate in the committee, the validation gate in Layer-2, the directional-accuracy gate in the signal service. When evidence is weak, the system falls back to the simple option. Source: exp6, exp32, exp73, exp70-MS.

4. The significance filter

A component ships only if it beats the current best significantly on paired tests. exp51 (the kitchen sink) is the validation: recombining all nonsignificant positives made the method worse everywhere, so the filter was keeping out noise, not gains.

5. Honest ranges

Ranges are calibrated conformally and reported with their width. Coverage without width is not a result; width without coverage is not either. Source: exp5, exp10, exp74, exp76.

6. Kill criteria are tripwires, not executions

A failed gate triggers diagnosis and 4 to 5 improvement rounds (ablations, parameter search, prompt tuning) before anything is called dead. The iteration log ships with each report. Source: docs/MACRO_SIGNAL.md; standing instruction 2026-10-19.

Appendix C

Glossary

The program's conventions, in the words the program uses them. Abridged from docs/METHODOLOGY.md section 0 where marked.

CRPSContinuous Ranked Probability Score: the primary accuracy metric. A proper score on the whole predicted distribution; lower is better. Rewards forecasts that are well-centered and honest about uncertainty.
PIT / as-ofPoint-in-time: a forecast for a date may use only information whose as-of date is on or before the forecast origin. Any feature, label, or context with observed_asof after the origin is a leak.
CoverageThe share of realized outcomes that land inside a stated interval. A nominal 80% interval should cover about 80% of outcomes; coverage without the accompanying width is not a result.
Conformal predictionMethods that set interval widths from past forecast errors so that coverage hits a target, with no distributional assumptions.
CQRConformalized quantile regression (Romano et al. 2019): calibrate a model's native quantile interval by adding a calibrated exceedance score computed on past origins. The default range layer in this report.
ACIAdaptive conformal inference (Gibbs and Candes 2021): the target level is updated online after each hit or miss. Long-run coverage converges even under arbitrary shift. Used here as a regime-gated option on volatile subsets.
Calibration bankThe set of past rolling-origin forecasts and their realized outcomes that a conformal method draws its scores from. The binding constraint of this program: per-series banks of 8 to 12 origins starve the machinery. exp81 (in flight) pools banks across series.
RegimeA mode of the data (seasonal, trending, noisy). Hand-written rules detect the regime; the router assigns one expert per regime and blends by regime probability.
Gate / vetoA rule that can refuse a component: the do-nothing gate falls back to the global best single model; the Layer-2 gate vetoes a forecaster in favor of naive; the signal gate admits a macro category only above 55% train accuracy.
Trust flagEASY / HARD tag on a forecast: the system reports not only a forecast but whether the evidence supports trusting it. exp32 measured a 7.2-point gap between the two classes.
Win rateThe fraction of scored origins (or series) where one method beats another head to head, reported alongside the paired p-value.
ShrinkagePulling an estimate toward a safer value: James-Stein shrinkage (k=0.5) damps agent adjustments; the Layer-2 shrink arm blends a group forecast toward the global one. Neither always helps; both are tested.
TSFMTime-series foundation model: Chronos-2 or TimesFM-2.5 here. Pretrained on many series, used zero-shot, not fine-tuned.
MASEMean absolute scaled error: the Monash table's metric for the Tourism benchmark (lower is better). Our seasonal naive scores 1.080 against published best DeepAR 1.409.
Point massA degenerate forecast that puts all probability on one value: the exp72/exp78 defect, where the group forecaster tiled the last value across every quantile level (coverage 0.000).
Lock-inThe difference between a borrower's existing rate and current market rates, tested as a runoff covariate in Layer-2. Carried no signal (|r| at most 0.043); the gated variant correctly stayed silent.
SMMSingle monthly mortality: the monthly rate at which loans leave a mortgage portfolio. The Freddie Mac benchmark's target.
RunoffThe decline in a mortgage portfolio's balances as loans mature, amortize, or prepay. The OSFI benchmark's target.
Blocked CVCross-validation with contiguous time blocks as folds. Respects the arrow of time; no block tests on data its fold was trained on.
VincentizationAveraging experts' quantiles level by level (the soft combination used inside the regime router, exp43).
World A / World BThe report's labeling of the two test worlds. World A: the synthetic mortgage book (SimulatorV2 and the shift battery). World B: public and real data (M4, M3, Tourism, Food CPI, BoC, WTI, SMM, OSFI). The two are never mixed in a table or chart.
